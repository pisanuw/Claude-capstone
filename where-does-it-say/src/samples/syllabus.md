# CS 142: Introduction to Programming, Autumn 2026

Lakeside College, School of Computing. This is a sample syllabus written for the Where Does It Say demo; the course, people and policies are fictional.

## Course staff

- Instructor: Dr. Rana Okafor, rokafor@lakeside.example.edu
- Teaching assistants: Mateo Ruiz and Priya Natarajan
- Course email: cs142-staff@lakeside.example.edu

Email the course address rather than an individual so that whoever is on duty can answer. We reply within one working day. Questions about grades must be sent from your college email account.

## Meetings

Lectures are on Monday and Wednesday, 10:30 to 11:50, in Hall 204. Lab sections meet on Thursday in the Maker Lab (room B12); you must attend the lab section you are registered for.

Office hours are Tuesday 2:00 to 4:00 (Dr. Okafor, room 318) and Friday 1:00 to 3:00 (TAs, Maker Lab). Office hours are drop-in; no appointment is needed.

## Textbook and software

There is no required textbook. Readings are posted on the course site each week. We use Python 3.12 and the Thonny editor, both free. You may use any computer that runs them; a lab machine is available if you do not have one.

## Grading

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Labs (8) | 25% |
| Weekly quizzes | 10% |
| Projects (2) | 25% |
| Midterm exam | 15% |
| Final exam | 25% |

Your lowest two quiz scores are dropped. Lab 0 is ungraded practice and does not count toward the lab average.

Letter grades follow the usual scale: 93% and above is an A, 90% to 92.9% is an A-, and each band below is three percentage points wide. The scale will not be made stricter, but it may be curved upward at the end of the term.

To pass the course you must score at least 50% on the combined exam average, whatever your overall total.

## Late work

Labs and projects are due at 11:59 pm on the due date shown on the course site. Late submissions lose 10% of the available points per day, up to three days. Work more than three days late receives a zero.

Every student has three free late days for the term. A late day extends a lab or project deadline by 24 hours with no penalty. Late days are applied automatically, in order, to the first late submissions; you cannot choose which assignment they cover. Late days cannot be used for quizzes or exams.

Extensions beyond your late days are granted only for documented emergencies. Ask for one by email before the deadline if at all possible.

## Exams

The midterm is on Wednesday, October 28, in the lecture room. The final exam is on Tuesday, December 15, 8:00 to 10:00 am, in the Main Gym.

Exams are closed book. You may bring one letter-size sheet of handwritten notes, both sides. Calculators, phones and laptops are not allowed during exams.

If you miss the midterm for a documented reason, the final exam counts for the midterm's weight as well. There is no make-up midterm. A missed final exam must be discussed with Dr. Okafor within 48 hours.

## Regrades

Regrade requests go through the course site within one week of the grade being released. Explain what you think was marked wrong. The whole submission may be regraded, so your score can go down as well as up.

## Attendance

Lecture attendance is not recorded. Lab attendance is: each lab is checked off in person during your section, and a lab that is not checked off during a section receives half credit at most. You may miss one lab section with no penalty; send the staff email before the section.

## Accommodations

Students with a letter from the Disability Resource Center should send it to Dr. Okafor in the first two weeks of term. Exam accommodations are arranged with the testing center at least one week before each exam.

## Communication

Announcements are posted on the course site and emailed to your college address. The discussion forum is the best place for questions about labs and projects; do not post your own solution code publicly on the forum.
